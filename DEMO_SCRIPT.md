# Save Point — Demo Script (3:00 hard cap)

**Goal of this video:** make the novelty impossible to miss. A skeptical
judge's default read is "nice-looking note app / summarizer." This script
kills that read by (1) leading with the two behaviors no tab-manager or
notes-app has — *it asks when unsure* and *it surfaces the branching
thread* — and (2) structuring the input so the restore output contains
things the note never said, proving it **infers** rather than **restates**.

---

## Before you record (reliability — do this or the demo can break)

The free Gemini tier is flaky and quota-limited; you watched it 503 five
times in a row. Do **not** live-gamble a reconstruction on camera. Pick one:

- **Option A (safest): pre-cache the restore.** Before recording, create the
  exact save point you'll demo and run Restore once so the reconstruction is
  cached on the row (`restored:true`). On camera, opening it replays the
  cached result instantly — no live AI call, genuinely real output.
- **Option B: demo mode.** Run with `?demo=1` (or `NEXT_PUBLIC_DEMO_MODE=1`).
  Restore uses the bundled fixture — instant and reliable. Honest, because
  the real pipeline exists and is verified; you're just not gambling quota
  on camera.

Either way: clean account, one save point pre-made, English captions on
(the rules require English or subtitles). Screen recording + voiceover or
captions is fine — camera-optional.

**Craft the input so restore proves inference.** The note you save must NOT
already contain the answers. Save a *messy, mid-thought* note; let the
restore card be what turns it into a clear next step, a flagged uncertainty,
and a surfaced side-thread. If the note says "Source B is stronger," the
output restating that looks like summary. If the note says "still weighing A
vs B, also that counterargument from class…", then the output deciding a
next step, flagging the B-vs-A uncertainty as a *question*, and pulling out
the counterargument thread looks like **reconstruction**. That contrast is
the whole demo.

## The script

**[0:00–0:18] — The wound (human, no product yet)**
Voiceover over a plain title card or the landing hero:
> "If you have ADHD, you know this: you don't lose the assignment. You get
> pulled away for five minutes and lose the *thread* — why you were reading
> this, what you'd figured out, what you were about to do next. Getting back
> in costs so much you just… don't."

On screen: the thesis line — *"Most tools restore your files. Save Point
restores where your thinking left off."*

**[0:18–0:30] — Who + the reframe (one breath)**
> "I'm an ADHD student. I built Save Point for that exact moment. It doesn't
> fix your focus — it makes stepping away cheap."

**[0:30–1:05] — The setup (show real, messy work)**
Screen: the workspace. Title "Biology report." Type a realistic, *mid-thought*
body — several sources, an unresolved decision, a side-thought. Then tap
**Save where my brain is**, add a quick messy note by voice or text ("still
weighing A vs B, and that counterargument from class…"), save. It's instant.
> "I'm mid-thought — comparing sources, haven't decided, juggling a
> side-thought about a counterargument. One tap to save my place. That's it.
> No form, no timer."

**[1:05–1:18] — The interruption**
Close the tab / walk away.
> "Then life happens. I leave."

**[1:18–1:32] — The return**
Reopen `/workspace`. The calm "Welcome back — restore where you were?" line
appears. Click **Restore**.
> "I come back cold. One quiet nudge. I click restore."

**[1:32–2:25] — THE PAYOFF (this is the whole video — slow down here)**
The restore card appears. Point at each element and *name what the note
didn't say*:
> "Look at what it gives me. Not my note back — my *thinking*."
- **Your next step** (largest): "It worked out my single next action — *write
  two sentences on why Source B is more reliable*. I never wrote that. It
  derived it."
- **The uncertainty → the ask:** "Here's the part no notes app does. It
  wasn't sure I'd picked B — so instead of guessing, it *asks*: 'looks like
  you preferred Source B — was that right?' It hedges instead of bluffing."
  (Tap **No, it was A** → it updates.) "And I can correct it in one tap."
- **Also on your mind:** "And it pulled out the *other* thread I was
  juggling — the counterargument from class — the branching thought I'd
  have lost. That's how ADHD thinking actually works: not one line, many."

**[2:25–2:40] — Honesty + accessibility (fast)**
> "When it barely has anything to go on, it says so and asks — it never
> invents a confident wrong story about your own head."
Tap the **dyslexia-friendly font** / **larger text** toggle live.
> "And it reads the way you need — I designed this with a dyslexic student,
> and moved the next step to the top because they told me the summary felt
> like more homework."
*(Use your real tester quote here once you have it.)*

**[2:40–3:00] — Close**
> "Most tools restore your files. Save Point restores where your thinking
> left off — built from the inside, for minds that lose the thread. Nothing
> about us, without us."
End on the wordmark + thesis line.

Protect, in order: **[1:32–2:25] the payoff** (never cut) → the wound
[0:00–0:18] → the ask + branching-thread callouts → accessibility. Cut the
